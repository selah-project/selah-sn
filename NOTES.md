## §1 — Why Shona, and what cleared it

Shona sits at rank 100 in THE 127 with 7.5 million speakers in Zimbabwe and
the diaspora — Bantu S10, the standard orthography settled in 1967 on a
Zezuru/Karanga base.

It was reached in strict rank order. **Tajik at rank 91 was skipped**, not
passed over by accident: it carries a script-home flag (Cyrillic Tajik against
Perso-Arabic Persian is a distinct chair, and Western Punjabi at 40 million has
the prior claim on that ruling). The question is filed and is Scott's.

Two floors were cleared before a single verse burned:

- **the round-trip noun probe — 19 of 20.** Does the model invent the word?
- **the stability probe — 69%, verbs at 71%** (`exp 1026`). Asked twice,
  independently, does it give the *same* word?

The second floor is new, and Shona is the first chair lit with it in hand. It
exists because **chair 99 (Ilocano) passed the noun floor at 17 of 20 and then
cost a day of pins** — not because it lacked vocabulary, but because it would
not settle: `נבא` came out in 48 renderings across five stems, `צדק` in 79
forms, `משפט` in 58. The model *knew* the right words and used them correctly
wherever they appeared. **Consistency is a separate property from correctness**,
and the noun floor cannot see it.

## §2 — The register: why `Erohimu` and `Yahwe`

Two facts about standard Shona set the whole Name table.

**Shona has no `l`.** The standard orthography uses `r` where neighbouring
languages write `l`. So `אלהים` cannot be *Elohim* here; it is **`Erohimu`**.

**Shona builds open syllables** — every syllable ends in a vowel. So a
transliteration cannot end on a consonant or stack two. `יהוה` is **`Yahwe`**,
not *Yahweh*; `צבאות` is **`Tsevaoti`**; `אל` is **`Er`**.

This is not cosmetic and it has already had a consequence. **The first-hour
gate could not be copied from chair 99, because ilo's erasure class lists
`Yahwe` as a violation** — Ilocano's certified form is `Yahweh`, so `Yahwe`
there means the Name was truncated. Here `Yahwe` *is* the right answer. A
copied gate would have convicted the correct rendering at every one of the
6,830 seats the Name occupies.

### `Mwari` keeps a lawful seat, in lower case

`Mwari` is the ordinary Shona word for God and the single most important
rejection in the rails. **It is not a bad word — it is the right word. But it
is not the Name.** If `יהוה` or `אלהים` becomes `Mwari`, the reader cannot see
what the Hebrew said.

But **`mwari` / `vamwari` in lower case is correct** where the text means the
gods of the nations — the golden calf of Exodus 32:8, Baal-zebub of Ekron,
Dagon, the gods of Syria. Transliteration is reserved for the One; the native
word does the work for the others.

Chair 99 arrived at exactly this pattern **unasked**, using `dios` in 181
places and every one of them a false god. The rails here state it rather than
hoping for it. And it is why **the gate's erasure test is case-sensitive**: the
fault is `Mwari` capitalised standing where the Name belongs, not the word
itself.

One known false positive, written down before it fired: a lawful lowercase
`mwari` beginning a sentence is capitalised by orthography, not by register —
*Mwari weEkironi*, the god of Ekron. Those are verses to read, not convictions.

## §4 — The first two pins, found by reading three verses

Both were found at 01:07, four minutes after the fire was lit, by reading
Genesis 1:1, 1:2 and 1:26 by eye. Both are of the class that needs no native
ear, because **the Hebrew surface and the wrong gloss settle it between them.**

### `פני` → `tsigiri` is *foundation*, not face

Genesis 1:2 read `pamusoro petsigiri yemvura` — **above the foundation of the
water** — and again in the same verse for `על־פני תהום`. `tsigiri` means a
foundation or support. Shona for face is **`chiso`**.

`פני` runs past two thousand occurrences in the Tanakh, which made this the
largest pin available in the first hour by a wide margin. Pinned flat with
`tsigiri` forbidden by name.

*Confirmed taking:* by gate 3, `tsigiri` survived in exactly one file —
Genesis 1:2 itself, written three minutes before the pin — and `chiso` had
appeared in ten. By gate 3 `chiso` was showing up in the loop-probe reading
list at Genesis 32:21, where the Hebrew genuinely repeats פנים three times.

### `דגת` → `mhodzi` is *seed*, not fish

Genesis 1:26 had them ruling over **the seeds of the sea**. Shona for fish is
**`hove`**.

### And `mhodzi` immediately taught the lesson back

It looked like a five-file fault. **Three of the five were correct.** `mhodzi`
*is* Shona for seed, and it was standing over `זרע` in Genesis 1:11, 1:12 and
15:5, where seed is exactly what the Hebrew says. Only the two `בדגת` hits were
wrong, and both predated the pin.

So **a word that is lawful over a different Hebrew root cannot go on a
flow-level watch list** — it must be watched at the token, paired with its
surface. The gate grew `pin-pairs` for this, a check chair 99 does not have.

The gate's thermometer watches the four neighbours the rails name: Ndebele,
Zulu, Chichewa and English.

**The hazard here is the inverse of Ilocano's, and worse.** Ilocano's
neighbours are Tagalog and Spanish — different families, so a word list mostly
discriminates. **Shona's neighbours are all Bantu, and Bantu languages share

wholesale.

Struck before the first run:

|---|---|---|
| `usiku` | Chichewa and Swahili, *night* | **it is also the Shona** |
| `nyama` | Chichewa, *meat* | **it is also the Shona** |
| `munthu` | Chichewa, *person* | Shona is `munhu` — **one letter apart** |

**`ndipo`.** Chichewa for *and then* — and also ordinary Shona, `ndi-` plus the

This is chair 99's `pilak` again, one gate earlier — and worse, because the
comment directly above the list in the same file already warned that Bantu

against it.**

## §6 — The innocence assert caught a prohibition and called it a certification

The gate refuses to start if any probe list accuses a word the rails certify.
On its first run it refused, naming **`tsigiri`**.

`tsigiri` is not certified. It is **forbidden**, and the rails say so in the
same breath — `kwete tsigiri` (Shona *kwete* = not). But the rails also bold it
in a heading, and a bare `**word**` scrape cannot tell a certification from a
prohibition.

That is not a Shona problem. **A forbidden word is the single most natural
thing to put on a watch list** — the watch list exists to catch exactly the
strings the rails forbid — so this would have fired on every pin ever added, on
every chair. It never surfaced on chair 99 only because that chair's forbids
happened never to be bolded alone.

The fix is in the extractor, not the rails: a bold word within short reach
after `kwete` or `NOT` is a prohibition, and is excluded.

## §7 — The fabricated ⟨את⟩ is a backward copy

The gate's one non-zero structural class, and it was growing — one hit at 791
files, three at 884, which makes it a rate rather than a residue.

Reading all of them showed the class has been **misnamed**, here and possibly
fleet-wide. The marker is not invented. The verse's real את is present and
correctly marked, and the marker has *also* been copied onto an earlier token:

| verse | the copy | the source |
|---|---|---|
| Genesis 31:34 | `לקחה` → `⟨את⟩ atora` | `את` → `⟨את⟩`, next token |
| Genesis 31:34 | `לבן` → `⟨את⟩ Labhani` | `את` → `⟨את⟩`, next token |
| Genesis 21:2 | `אשר` → `yaakataura ⟨את⟩` | `אתו` → `⟨את⟩`, two later |

Genesis 21:2 fixes the shape: `אשר` carries **דבר's gloss as well as אתו's
marker**, so a whole span of the row slid left and the marker rode along.

Why it matters: **an invented marker is a judgment; a copy is arithmetic.** If
a token carries the marker, holds no את-family letters, and a later token in
the verse both holds את and is marked, the first is a duplicate and stripping it
is settled without reading the verse. `exp 1027` classifies them. At 1,099
files: **three backward-copy, one not.**

**A strip is only safe when something is left standing.** A token whose gloss is
*only* the marker is never stripped — that is the `2 Chronicles 35:22` seat from
chair 99, where a bare strip would have traded a fabricated marker for an empty
gloss.

## §9 — Genesis 5:27, where the row acquits and the flow convicts

```
FLOW: Mazuva ese aya aya emadzinza emunhu — kopivosi — akaita makore
      mazana mapfumbamwe namakumi matanhatu nepfumbamwe, obva afa.
ROW:  מתושלח → Metuseraki
```

**The row is right.** Methuselah is correctly transliterated. **The flow
dropped the name and put noise in its place** — `— kopivosi —`.

The number beside it is correct: `mazana mapfumbamwe` (900) + `makumi
matanhatu` (60) + `pfumbamwe` (9) = **969**, which is Methuselah's age. And
`ימי` carries a bloated `mazuva aya aya emadzinza emunhu`, with `aya` doubled.

**Read both the row and the flow.** On chair 99 the standing example was
Exodus 7:7, where the row was fixed and the flow kept the wrong number, so the
reader met a wrong age in the sentence. This is the same fault inverted — and
it is the reader-facing half that is wrong, which is the worse direction.

Seated for the re-press; a dropped name in the flow is not a scriptable repair.

Genesis 31:34 also shows a **doubled source token** — `וורחל` with two vavs,
followed by `ורחל` — and the chair rendered both as `Rachero`. `וורחל` is not a
Hebrew word. Chair 99 met the same class at `job/38/37`'s doubled samekh
(`יסספר`) and `1-kings/20/28`'s `ויאא`, where **the source token is malformed
and the floor has no gloss either** — the one category belonging to neither the
chair nor the rails.

## §10 — The gate's readings, which are not convictions

Recorded so they are not mistaken for faults later:

- **LOOPS** — a word repeated in the flow more often than the Hebrew repeats
  anything. Needs no vocabulary and works in any chair. Shona makes this
  noisier than Ilocano did, because **Shona fuses its function words as concord
  prefixes** (`ne-` with, `pa-` at, `ku-` to, `-ye/-wa/-dze/-re` of), so
  agreement causes natural repetition within a verse. Genesis 32:21's `chiso 3`
  is the Hebrew repeating פנים, not the chair looping.
- **NON-LATIN** — single Cyrillic and Greek letters hiding inside Shona words
  (`а`, `вен`, `к`, `χ`). Real, and the same class as the homoglyph sweep
  (`exp 1023`). These are form faults, seated for repair.
- **ACCENTED LETTERS** — standard Shona uses no diacritics, so `Pherégi` (Peleg)
  and `yeKanáana` carry stray accents. Real, small, seated.

## §12 — Exodus 11:6–8 is in Yiddish, and the gate could not see it

Gate 4 reported `NON-LATIN 13 verses` with its sample list capped at twelve, so
`exodus/11/7` sat in that list looking exactly like `genesis/27/3`, whose entire
fault is one Cyrillic `а` hiding inside a Shona word.

**Exodus 11:6, 11:7 and 11:8 are rendered entirely in Yiddish**, row and flow:

```
אָבער קעגן אלע קינדער פֿון ישראל וועט קיין הונט נישט אויפֿשאַרפֿן זײַן צונג…
```

A contiguous run of three verses where the model left the language altogether.

**And the gate caught it only by accident.** The Name line flagged
`exodus/11/7 יהוה → יהוה` because the Name was passed through unglossed and so
failed the `Yahwe` test. Had the chair written the Name as Yiddish does, **a
verse that is one hundred percent wrong would have scored clean on every line of

and one more entry in a capped NON-LATIN sample changes nothing.

So the gate grew `alien-share`: the **fraction** of a flow's letters that are
not Latin, reported for any verse above a third. A count of offending
*characters* cannot tell one stray letter from a whole verse in another
language, **and those are not the same fault** — the first is a homoglyph to
repair, the second is a verse to re-press. It now reads:

```

ALIEN exodus/11/7 100%
ALIEN exodus/11/8 100%
ALIEN verses >34% non-Latin  3
```

Three verses, isolated exactly, and separated from the thirteen single-character
homoglyphs they were hiding among.

This is written down as a class, not as four incidents, because the pattern is
the point.

cannot be watched in the flow — the flow carries no Hebrew, so nothing in it can

written the correct reasoning in a comment before coding the opposite.

1. **`mhodzi`** — watched at the flow, five hits, three of them lawful over

2. **`ndipo`** — put on the bleed list directly beneath my own paragraph warning
   that Bantu neighbours share roots. Three hits, three lawful.
3. **`Tenzi`** — left in the flow erasure class, which read 2 verses for the
   first time on gate 4. Both lawful: `אדני → Tenzi wangu` is Judah addressing
   Joseph, a **human** lord, and `אדניו → tenzi wake` is Potiphar, **lowercase

4. **`Mwari`** — and then I wrote `(?i)` into that very pair, after arguing in

The instrument is now: flow checks hold only what has no lawful seat in any
Shona sentence; anything a Hebrew surface can arbitrate is checked at the token.

## §14 — The unit of failure is the CALL, not the verse

The three Yiddish verses all carry **the same mtime second — 01:28:07** — and so
does `exodus/11/5`. Four files. The batch-size distribution across the corpus is
**522 batches of exactly 4**, which is `relay-move-on!`'s batch size, so
mtime-grouping recovers the call boundary reliably.

**So one call produced all four.** `11/5` came out in Shona; `11/6`, `11/7` and
`11/8` went to Yiddish, and the model never recovered inside the call.

| file | mtime | |
|---|---|---|
| exodus/11/3 | 01:27:57 | Shona — previous call |
| exodus/11/4 | 01:27:57 | Shona — previous call |
| **exodus/11/5** | **01:28:07** | **Shona, but see below** |
| **exodus/11/6** | **01:28:07** | **Yiddish, 100%** |
| **exodus/11/7** | **01:28:07** | **Yiddish, 100%** |
| **exodus/11/8** | **01:28:07** | **Yiddish, 100%** |
| exodus/11/9 | 01:26:36 | Shona — the press is not sequential |

**And `11/5` is not clean either.** It renders the firstborn as `vachembero` —
Shona for firstborn is `dangwe` (plural `matangwe`), while `vachembere` means
**the elderly**. *By the death of the elderly* where the Hebrew says *every
firstborn*.

So the drift **began before the script switched**, and the script-share measure
catches the **tail** of the failure rather than its head. The first verse of the
call was already wrong; it was wrong in *meaning*, which no structural check can
see, and only the later verses were wrong in a way a machine can spot.

**The operating rule, which the gate now prints:** when a whole-verse script
substitution is found, **re-press its entire call**, identified by shared mtime
second — not only the flagged verses. Three were flagged; four must be
re-pressed.

*This rests on one call. It is written as a rule because the batch arithmetic is
exact and the remedy costs nothing extra, but the mechanism — that a drift always
starts before it becomes visible — is a hypothesis until a second call agrees.*

**Not live.** No alien verse has appeared since 01:28:07, with roughly twelve
hundred verses burned after it. This is a single-call event, not a tendency, and
it needs no rails pin — the rails were not consulted differently for that call.

## §19 — Two pins, and only one of them can be said to have taken

Both went in within ten minutes of each other, and the arithmetic treats them
completely differently. **The rule is: a pin has not taken until enough verses
have burned that the prior rate would have produced a fault.** Silence in a
window too small to hold one is not evidence.

### The Name's casing — 03:37 — NOT PROVEN

`YaHwe` and `YaHWeH` stood at five instances: Numbers 30:17 (02:05),
Deuteronomy 28:13 and 28:15 (**02:18:53, one call**), 1 Kings 12:15 (03:12),
2 Kings 23:9 (03:27). The rails already stated `Yahwe`, so the pin states the
**casing** and forbids the wrong strings by name — reinforcement, not a new rule.

Prior rate: **5 in 9,819 = 0.05%.** Post-pin at 03:47: 410 verses, none.

**410 × 0.05% = 0.2 expected. So nothing has been shown.** About 2,000 clean
verses are needed before the absence carries any weight, and saying otherwise
would be reading a window as a result.

### No diacritics — 03:47 — CONSISTENT WITH TAKING

Prior rate: **66 verses in 10,261 = 0.64%.** Post-pin: **0 of 435.**

**435 × 0.64% = 2.8 expected, 0 observed.** That window is big enough to hold
several, and it holds none. **This is the first density arithmetic in the whole
burn that actually carries weight** — and it is one measurement, so it is
*consistent with the pin taking* and not yet a mechanism. Re-check at a thousand.

### Why the second one was a gap and the first was compliance

This is the distinction that decides whether a fault is the chair's or mine.

**The Name's casing was compliance.** The D1 table said `Yahwe` from the
beginning; the chair departed from a rule it had been given. Chair 99's number
faults were the same shape — the right table was on the rails and the chair wrote
sixty for eighty anyway.

**The diacritics were a GAP, and the gap was mine.** Nothing in the rails
mentioned accents. I had written *"standard Shona writes no accent marks"* into
`README.md` and `CONTRIBUTING.md` — the files a **reader** sees — and never into
the file the **model** reads. The rails listed the alphabet and forbade `l`, `q`
and `x`, and said nothing about `é`.

So the standing rule has a second half I had only been applying in one direction:
**check the rails before calling a fault a gap — and check them before calling a
gap a fault.** Seventy-nine accented words were sitting in the census as though
the chair had disobeyed, and it had never been told.

### The second measurement, and the two pins part company

**Diacritics — TAKEN.** Prior 0.73%, post-pin **0 of 966, expected 7.1**. The
first window expected 2.8 and held none; this one expects seven and holds none.
Two measurements agree, which is the bar this project sets for calling something a
mechanism rather than a hypothesis.

**The Name's casing — DID NOT TAKE, and the arithmetic says so plainly.**
`jeremiah/22/9`, written at 03:58:59, **twenty-one minutes after the pin**, has
`יהוה → YaHwe`. Post-pin: **1 of 1,476 where 0.8 was expected.** That is the prior
rate, unchanged. Not a reduced rate, not a disproved pin — **no detectable
effect.**

And the shape of the failure is worth keeping: **the row is wrong and the flow is
right.** The flow reads `sungano yaYahwe Erohimu yavo` — the possessive `ya-`
prefixed to a correct `Yahwe`. So the pin reached the sentence the reader meets and
not the row beneath it.

**That is the third row/flow divergence on this chair**, and each has gone a
different way: Genesis 5:27 has the row right (`Metuseraki`) and the flow carrying
noise; the Exodus 7:7 class on chair 99 had the row repaired and the flow left
wrong; this one has the flow right and the row wrong. **The two surfaces drift
independently and neither is reliably the better one.** Read both, always.

### And the judgment: this one does not get another pin

The pin laws say a frequent pin needs reinforcement. **This pin should not get
it.** The class is ~0.05% — about ten seats over the whole Tanakh — and it is
**fully deterministic for §9**: surface `יהוה`, gloss a Name variant and nothing
else, write `Yahwe`. exp 1028 already does it.

Rails text is not free. Every line of it rides in every prompt for every one of
23,213 verses, so a rule earns its place by what it prevents. **A 0.05% class that
a script repairs for nothing does not earn more room**, and reinforcing it would
crowd the rules that carry the Name, the marker and the register.

The statement stays where it is — removing it would be churn, and it costs little.
But the fix for this class is §9, and saying otherwise would be treating a pin as
the only tool because it is the one I reached for first.

### The third measurement, which takes "TAKEN" back

Two ticks ago I wrote **TAKEN** for the diacritics pin on two agreeing windows —
0 of 435 where 2.8 were expected, then 0 of 966 where 7.1 were expected. At
**3,115** post-pin verses it is **6, against 22.8 expected.**

**So the verdict was premature and I am withdrawing the word.** The pin cut the
class by roughly three quarters; it did not end it. Two windows agreeing is the
bar for a mechanism being *real*, and I read it as the bar for a class being
*closed*. Those are different claims and I made the second one.

**And the residual is a different shape from what the pin named**, which is the
part worth keeping. The pre-pin class was overwhelmingly an acute on a **vowel** in
a transliterated name — `Moshé`, `Pherégi`, `Shaúru`. The six residuals split:

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| `ružwi` | `וגוי`, *nation* | **`ž` on a consonant** — carrying `zh`/`dz`; the word wanted `rudzi` |
| `akašara` | `אכזרי`, *cruel* | **`š` on a consonant** — carrying `sh` |
| `⟨pası̇⟩` | `ארץ`, *earth* | **`ı̇`** — a Turkish dotless i with a combining dot above |
| `Ahorá` | `אהלה`, Aholah | an acute on a vowel — the old class |
| `chokuperfetí` | `כלילת` | an acute on a vowel, and `perfetí` looks like a Romance loan |
| `hári` | `לא` | an acute on a vowel |

**Three vowel, three not.** So the vowel class — the one the pin actually
describes — fell from ~0.7% to about 0.10%, which is the real measurement. The
other three are a class the pin *names* (`š č ž ñ ŵ` are forbidden by name in the
same paragraph) and has not reached, so that half is **non-compliance, not a gap**.

§9 handles the three vowel cases and correctly refuses the other three: a
diacritic on a consonant may be carrying a letter, and `ž` here is exactly that.
They go to §10.

**The lesson about the instrument, not the chair:** a pin's effect has to be
measured against *the shape the pin names*, not against every hit the detector
can see. My detector matched all of `À-ſ`, so it was scoring the pin on
three faults it never addressed — which made a 86% reduction on the named class
look like a 74% reduction on a vaguer one, and made zero look like closure.

## §20 — The bleed list's ninth hit is the first true one

Eight false convictions came out of this list, in two causes: six words that are
**also Shona** (`mhodzi` · `ndipo` · `Tenzi` · `Mwari` · the concord prefix · the
stem test) and two where the chair had **transliterated the Hebrew** and the result
collided with a Swahili word (`giza` for `גזה`, Gideon's fleece; `neno` for a
mis-split `ne-`).

After eight, the temptation is to strike the mechanism. **The ninth is real.**

**`ezekiel/44/19` — `בם` → `madzi`.** `בם` is *in them*, a preposition with a
pronominal suffix. The gloss is a noun meaning water. **It does not correspond to
its surface at any level**, so it is a fault whatever language `madzi` belongs to —
the bleed list only happened to be the thing that noticed.

**`proverbs/3/20` — `תהומות` → `madzi akadzima`**, for *the depths*. Standard
Shona for water is **`mvura`**; `madzi` is the Chichewa. The sense is near enough
that only an ear can rule, so it goes on the reading list rather than the fault
list.

### What this says about a probe with a 1-in-9 hit rate

**Its value is not its precision.** Eight of nine hits were wrong and the
mechanism still earned its place, because every one of the nine was **a verse
worth reading** — and six of the eight false ones taught something about Shona
that is now written into the gate (the concord prefix, the lawful human lord, the
lowercase god of the nations, the transliteration collision).

The rule this chair has confirmed nine times over: **a hit is a verse to read,
never a verse to convict.** A probe that is wrong eight times out of nine and
names its reasons is worth keeping. One that is right and cannot say why is not.

### And the Name class has stopped growing

17 faults at gate 11 (5,141 seats) and **17 at gate 12 (5,604 seats)** — none new
in 463 seats, where the 0.33% rate expects about 1.5. That is weakly consistent
with the 03:37 casing pin having had an effect after all, and **it is one window
and I am not claiming it.** The verdict stays where it was: no detectable effect,
and §9 repairs the class regardless.

### A tenth probe, abandoned before it was built — and the reason was already on file

`ezekiel/44/19`'s `בם → madzi` is a gloss that does not correspond to its surface
at any level, so I asked whether the class is measurable: Hebrew
preposition-plus-pronominal-suffix is a **closed set**, so a token whose surface is
one of those and whose gloss carries no Shona locative ought to be suspect.

It matched **9,209 of 15,975** — 58%. That is not a fault class; that is a regex.

**And both halves failed for reasons this project has already written down.**

The Hebrew side: the pattern caught `מי` (*who*), `כי` (*because*) and `עלה` (*he
went up*), because **unpointed `מי` is both *who* and `מ`+`י` (*from me*)**, and
`כי` is both *because* and `כ`+`י`. That is
`docs/findings/the-unpointed-collisions.md` exactly — eighteen families where the
consonants do not say which word it is — and the cure was named there and in
`exp 1016`: **count by lemma, not by letters.** A closed-set probe over an
unpointed skeleton is not a closed set at all.

The Shona side: `nahum/1/7`'s `בו → mairi` is **correct** — `mairi` is *in it* —
and my function-word list simply did not hold it. So even the verses the Hebrew
side selected rightly were being convicted by an incomplete inventory.

**Nothing was built and nothing is being shipped.** The reason is recorded because
a probe that fails for a documented cause is worth more written down than deleted:
the next person to reach for the closed-set idea on an unpointed text should find
this rather than rediscover it. `ezekiel/44/19` stays a single seat in the §10
list, found by reading, which is where it was found in the first place.

### The landing command, verified at 05:47 rather than at the moment

§6's first commit must be **the verse files alone**. Verified against the live
tree before the landing, not improvised during it:

```bash
cd ~/.selah/sources/translations/our-rendering/sn
git add $(ls -d */ | tr -d '/')      # 18,411 files — verse JSON only
# `git add .` stages 18,417 — the six extra are the root .md files
```

**The difference is exactly six**, which is the number of root documents
(`README` · `CONTRIBUTING` · `PROVENANCE` · `LICENSE` · `NOTES` ·
`LEXICON-QUESTIONS`). They stay untracked until after the landing commit so that
the first commit is the corpus exactly as the burn produced it and every later
correction reads as a diff against what the machine actually wrote.

This was checked in advance because **I committed 39 files that were not mine
earlier tonight** by reaching for `git add -A` on a directory instead of naming
paths. A command verified at 05:47 cannot be mistyped at 07:05.

Books on disk at 05:47: **34 of 39.** The five absent are Daniel, Ezra, Nehemiah
and both Chronicles — the tail of the Ketuvim, which matches the ~5,300 still
missing.

### A plateauing cumulative count is not a falling rate

Gates 11, 12 and 13 all reported the Name class at **17** — 5,141 then 5,604 then
5,671 seats with no new fault. I read that as the casing pin possibly having taken
after all, against my own published verdict, and went to measure it properly.

**Per seat, pre and post the 03:37 pin:**

```
PRE-pin   9 faults / 3,094 Name seats = 0.291%
POST-pin  6 faults / 2,581 Name seats = 0.232%   (expected 7.5, observed 6)
```

**A 20% difference on six observations is noise. "Did not take" stands.**

And the plateau has a dull cause: **all six post-pin faults are in Jeremiah and
Ezekiel, which were burned before gate 11.** The books burning since — the minor
prophets and the writings — simply carry fewer `יהוה` seats. The cumulative count
stopped moving because the *supply* of seats changed, not because the rate did.

So the law has an inverse worth writing beside it. *A growing count is a rate*
was learned on this project the hard way. **A plateauing cumulative count is not
a falling rate** — and it is the more seductive of the two, because it looks like
success.

The gate's 17 against my 15 is also reconciled: the gate counts an **empty** gloss
on a `יהוה` seat as a fault, and my scan required a non-empty gloss. The two
missing are empty-gloss Name seats, `2-kings/22/13` among them. Both instruments
are right about different questions.

### And a Name sub-class §9 cannot repair

`ezekiel/31/10` — `יהוה` → **`nokuti`**, which is *because*. Not a variant of the
Name, not a decorated form, not an honorific standing in its place: **the Name
replaced by a conjunction.** §9's rule matches Name *variants* and will leave this
untouched, correctly — there is nothing in the gloss to normalise. It is a
re-press seat, and it is the one Name fault in the corpus that is neither a form
error nor an erasure by an honorific.

### The landing is TWO commits, not one — caught at 06:26, not at 07:10

§9's `apply!` refuses to mutate the corpus unless the git tree is clean, so that
the whole repair pass is one reviewable diff and `git checkout .` reverts it.
**`git status --porcelain` counts untracked files**, and the six root documents are
untracked by design. So after a verse-files-only landing commit the tree still
shows six lines and **`apply!` would have thrown at the moment** — with the
obvious temptation being `:force`, which is exactly the guard the flag exists to
defeat.

So the landing is **two commits**, which is the right order regardless:

```bash
cd ~/.selah/sources/translations/our-rendering/sn
git add $(ls -d */ | tr -d '/')   # 1. the corpus, exactly as the burn made it
git commit -m "sn — Shona, chair 100: the landing"
git add *.md                      # 2. the root documents (§12)
git commit -m "sn — the root documents"
# tree now clean; §9 can run and be read as one diff
```

**The first commit must carry no document**, because its whole purpose is to be
the baseline every later correction diffs against. The second exists so the guard
on the third can do its job.

This is the second thing about the landing verified in advance rather than
improvised: the staging command at 05:56, the commit count at 06:26. Both were
checked because **a mistake at the landing is a mistake in the one commit that is
supposed to be the honest record of what the machine wrote.**

## §21 — The burn has two passes, and both signals agree

`relay-move-on!` is documented as **one full pass at batch 4, one retry at batch 2,
then move on with residue recorded.** Two independent measurements say the retry
pass began somewhere around 06:30.

**The gap distribution.** At 06:46, with 1,958 verses outstanding, they were spread
across **37 of 39 books** — psalms 232, jeremiah 145, ezekiel 144, isaiah 130,
genesis 94 — at roughly 5–15% of each. Not concentrated in the last book. So the
first pass did not complete a book before moving on; it left gaps everywhere and
something is now closing them.

**The rate.** It held at **~67/min for ten hours**, across a dozen samples from
01:03 to 06:36. Between 06:46 and 06:56 it was **497 verses in ten minutes —
49.7/min.** A retry at batch 2 moves half as many verses per call as a pass at
batch 4, and a drop of roughly a third is what that predicts once the
per-call overhead is counted.

**Two signals, and they were measured for different reasons**, which is the bar
this chair has been holding all night. The distribution was checked to find out
whether the last book was in progress; the rate was checked because the landing
estimate kept slipping. They agree.

**What it means for the landing.** §4's proof is *`count-missing` → 0, **or the
residue number written down***. If the batch-2 retry cannot fill a verse, it stays
unfilled and the relay moves on — so **the final number may not be zero, and the
right action is to write it down rather than wait for a zero that is not coming.**

And my earlier read was wrong in a way worth keeping: at 05:56 I noted five absent
book directories and said they were "the tail of the Ketuvim, which matches the
~5,300 still missing." That was a fair reading of what was visible — the five books
really were absent — **but it implied a sequential burn, and the burn is not
sequential across books any more than it is within them.** The known law was the
narrower one.
